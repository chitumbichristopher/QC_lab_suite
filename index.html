How to Prepare & Standardize a Volumetric Solution

General workflow applicable to most titrant solutions used in QC titrimetry

A. Preparation

  1. Calculate the theoretical weight (or volume, for liquid reagents) needed to reach the target molarity/normality using the calculator below.
  2. Weigh the reagent on a calibrated analytical balance, recording the exact weight taken.
  3. Dissolve in a suitable volume of the specified solvent (usually purified/distilled water) in a beaker, stirring until fully dissolved.
  4. Quantitatively transfer the solution into a volumetric flask of the correct target volume, rinsing the beaker into the flask.
  5. Make up to the calibration mark with solvent, stopper, and mix thoroughly by repeated inversion.
  6. Transfer to an appropriately labelled, dated amber or clear reagent bottle depending on light sensitivity; label with concentration, preparation date, analyst initials, and expiry/re-standardization due date.

B. Standardization

  1. Accurately weigh a primary standard of known high purity (see reference table below for the correct standard).
  2. Dissolve the primary standard in the appropriate solvent and add the specified indicator, or set up potentiometric/electrochemical endpoint detection where applicable.
  3. Titrate with the prepared volumetric solution to the defined endpoint, recording the volume consumed.
  4. Repeat in at least triplicate; the %RSD between replicate factor values should typically be ≤ 2.0% (use the %RSD calculator in the Calculators tab).
  5. Calculate the standardization factor (F) using the Standardization Factor calculator below.
  6. Record the mean factor, apply it to subsequent titration results, and set a re-standardization frequency per your site's SOP (commonly weekly to monthly, or before use if the solution is unstable).

Preparing Volumetric Solutions from Liquid (Concentrated) Chemicals

For reagents supplied as concentrated liquids (acids, ammonia solution, etc.) rather than solids

Why liquids are handled differently

Concentrated liquid reagents (e.g. Hydrochloric Acid, Sulfuric Acid, Acetic Acid, Ammonia solution) are not 100% pure compound — they are sold at a stated assay % (w/w) and have a known specific gravity. Both values (printed on the reagent bottle / Certificate of Analysis) are needed to work out how much of the liquid actually contains the required weight of active substance.

General procedure

  1. Record the assay % and specific gravity from the bottle label/CoA for that lot.
  2. Use the Liquid Reagent Dilution Calculator below to work out the volume of concentrated liquid required.
  3. Measure roughly half the target volume of solvent (usually purified water) into a beaker or volumetric flask first.
  4. Slowly add the measured concentrated liquid to the water — never water into concentrated acid — swirling/stirring to dissipate heat.
  5. Cool to room temperature, transfer to the volumetric flask, and make up to volume with solvent.
  6. Mix thoroughly, label, and standardize against the appropriate primary standard as described above before use.

Junior Analyst Topic: HPLC Mobile Phase

How long can buffered mobile phases be kept on the HPLC instrument before microbial growth becomes an issue?

Answer from QC Specialist:

A standard rule of thumb for aqueous buffer solutions without organic solvents is 24 to 48 hours max. Buffers with ≥ 20% organic phase can last up to 1-2 weeks in a sealed reservoir.
